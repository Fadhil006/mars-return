# viz-ux: presentation review, round 1

Screenshots (all in `scratchpad/shots/`): `viz-ux-d-0.png` (desktop, first load), `viz-ux-d- plan.png`, `viz-ux-d- 3d.png`, `viz-ux-d- rehearse.png`, `viz-ux-d- report.png`, `viz-ux-m-A.png` / `viz-ux-m-B.png` (390 px phone). These were driven over CDP using `scratchpad/vizux/cdp.mjs`.
The phone screenshot that stayed on "Loading the region" was a `--virtual-time-budget` artefact. With real time, the phone loads in under 4 s.

## 1. The 10-second verdict

It is a competent, honest dashboard, but nothing in it makes a judge gasp. It looks like a well-built internal tool, while the competitors look like a trip to Mars.

- **The first frame is a 3 x 3 km brown square floating in a dark void.** The DEM is only 150 x 150 cells at 20 m. Jezero, the delta, the crater rim and the place on Mars where this sits never appear. A judge cannot tell this is Jezero rather than any orange noise texture. Protikriti opens on a CesiumJS globe, so their first frame already says "Mars".
- **The 3D view has no moment of awe.** At 20 m per cell the floor of Jezero is a gently wavy slab: the elevation profile spans about 22 m over 2.2 km. The route is close to a straight line between two markers. Even at 2.5x auto relief it reads as a lumpy table. There is a visible artefact at the far edge: a hatched black spike, probably the missing-data mask extruded by the relief. Contour lines at 10 m cut through flat ground and add noise rather than meaning. There is no sky, horizon, haze or atmospheric perspective, so the slab ends in a hard cut and the eye reads "model", not "place".
- **The hierarchy is inverted.** The header is a two-row app bar (`MARS / EXCURSION PLANNER`, `Mission Control`, a date, a status pill, dataset hashes). It takes about 100 px on desktop and about 40% of the first phone screen. The map takes about 35% of the desktop width (480 to 820 px, letterboxed inside its column). In the plan state the rehearsal strip pushes the map down to about 560 px. The hero element is the smallest thing on the page.
- **The money moment, "Can we still get back?", is hidden.** It sits behind Rehearse, then Play, then Pause, then ticking a checkbox, then "Request return options". In a 3-minute look no judge does all five. The most dramatic number on screen is "Remaining margin 1 h 16 m" in green, which is the comfortable, undramatic case.
- **Density is in the wrong places.** The right column opens on Evidence: a scrolling table with a horizontal scrollbar, followed by paragraphs. The labelling is honest, but it is the wall of text a judge skips. Monospace dataset hashes in the header are developer chrome.
- **Typography is two-voice and fussy.** The header uses Inter at weight 300 in tracked uppercase, then 700 for "Mission Control". The assumptions use monospace with letter-spaced units (`30 m/min`, `15 °`). Five type treatments compete within 300 px, and nothing has a typographic "hero number" moment except the margin card.
- **Labels collide.** On 2D desktop the Legend covers Brac and Issole. On the phone the Legend covers four sites, the scale bar reads "250500", the Sid label is clipped off the right edge, and there is an empty 120 px gap in the header.
- **There is no motion grammar.** Nothing animates on load. The route appears instantly, the camera never moves on its own, and the base and sites are flat white dots. Real mission and visualization tools use motion to explain things; here motion exists only in the playback.
- **Why a judge would score Protikriti or MarsWay higher on Presentation:** a globe-to-surface zoom, a "rover street view" or first-person EVA view, and about 25 datasets of visual richness. MARS RETURN wins on Validity, but the Presentation criterion carries equal weight ([Judging & Awards Guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide)).

## 2. What the best do better

- **NASA Eyes on the Solar System**, Aug 2026 release ([release notes](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/eyes/2026/09/03/nasas-eyes-software-august-release-notes/)): full 3D Mars terrain, "land on the surface" and free-fly. What makes it work is the continuous zoom from orbit to the surface, so the place is established before the detail. Every NASA viewer opens with *context, then descent*.
- **Explore with Perseverance** ([JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/take-a-3d-spin-on-mars-and-track-nasas-perseverance-rover/)): HiRISE orbital terrain fused with rover imagery, plus split-screen of 3D and the actual images from that site. The lesson is to pair the model with a real photograph from the spot.
- **USGS Mars 2020 TRN HiRISE DTM mosaic, 1 m, and orthomosaic** ([DTM, doi:10.5066/P9REJ9JN](https://www.usgs.gov/data/mars-2020-terrain-relative-navigation-hirise-dtm-mosaic); [ortho, doi:10.5066/P9QJDP48](https://www.usgs.gov/data/mars-2020-terrain-relative-navigation-hirise-orthorectified-image-mosaic)). This is the product Perseverance itself landed on: 20x the linear resolution of the CTX DEM now in use, and public. The visual gap between 20 m and 1 m is the gap between "a slab" and "boulders, ridges and ripples".
- **Open MCT** ([NASA](https://nasa.github.io/openmct/about-open-mct); [User Guide, Time Conductor](https://nasa.github.io/openmct/docs/static/files/Open_MCT_Users_Guide.pdf)): real ops software is built around one global time conductor that every view obeys, with plots, imagery and events on one shared timeline. The rehearsal strip is halfway there but is not the organising spine of the UI.
- **Atmospheric perspective and sky-matched fog** ([Cinevva open-world write-up, 2026](https://app.cinevva.com/blog/2026-02-25-open-world-browser-part-12-lessons)): fade distant terrain into the actual sky colour with a depth smoothstep. This is a few lines in the existing `onBeforeCompile` shader. SSAO/HBAO ([realism-effects](https://unpkg.com/realism-effects-fork@1.1.4/readme.md)) gives crater rims contact shadows.
- **NYT/Pudding scrollytelling and Apple Maps Flyover**: scripted, eased camera paths with *cut points*. Each beat is a camera pose plus one sentence. That is exactly a demo video storyboard.

## 4. Demo video (how it should look)

**Verify the length first.** The official guide requires a demo with English subtitles hosted publicly ([Submission guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/)). Earlier Space Apps rounds capped the demo at about 30 s video or about 7 slides; I could not fetch the 2026 page to confirm. If a cap exists, cut a 30 s hero version and link the 2–3 min version separately.

For a 2–3 min structure, record each shot at 60 fps and 1440p, and use cuts rather than UI clicking:

1. **0:00–0:12.** Black frame, then the Jezero context descent (#1), with title text over it. Optionally one real Perseverance raw image as a cold open.
2. **0:12–0:35.** The question, in one sentence on screen. Pick Sid, routes draw on (animated stroke), and the camera pulls up.
3. **0:35–1:10.** Rehearsal: the time conductor plays, the camera follows the walker over the HiRISE terrain at low sun, and the HUD margin ticks down.
4. **1:10–1:50.** The event: "corridor blocked". The ribbon recolours, the point-of-no-return marker drops, and "Return now" is compared with "Continue then return" side by side.
5. **1:50–2:20.** Ask the assistant. The camera flies to the beat it chose, with citation chips highlighted. Say "it cannot invent numbers" and show the check.
6. **2:20–2:45.** Evidence montage: CTX, HiRISE, rover sols, MSL RAD. Then the report PDF, the honest limitations line, and the end card.

Production rules: no mouse-cursor hunting (pre-script the camera poses), burned-in subtitles, one music bed, and every hypothetical label stays visible in frame. Never speed-ramp a computed result.

## 5. The trap from another discipline

**"Add a first-person astronaut street view / walk mode"**, which the panel is likely to push to match MarsWay and Protikriti. On a 20 m DEM, or even 1 m, a ground-level camera shows smeared texels and stretched triangles. It looks worse than the orbital oblique and invites the "this isn't real" reaction. It also implies a validated surface view the data cannot support. Use real rover imagery at the sites (#6) for ground level, and keep the 3D camera above roughly 150 m altitude, where orbital data looks its best.
